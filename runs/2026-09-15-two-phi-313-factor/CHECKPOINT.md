# We have not solved the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer problem yet.

## H18: two Phi steps introduce 313, but not as the whole squareclass

Continuation of H17 from the same 137 seed and the authenticated H12/H13 osculating map `Phi`. H17 proved that no arbitrary linear/odd-multiplier preword followed by **one** Phi can reach squareclass 313 from the original seed. H18 tests the smallest genuinely nonlinear escape required by that obstruction: apply Phi once, then apply Phi again.

    y1=210069683/77723044.

       /7408174651270674517482423318520541589010497936550074743957818416506,

and an exact rational y2 and W2 preserved in `results/exact-values.txt` and regenerated by `verify.py`. Both applications remain on the same H13 genus-one fiber, with

    C=-39785/37632.

All denominators used by the full rational formulas are nonzero, and the final surface equation is checked exactly.

### 2. The target-prime local escape is real

Modulo 313, the exact rational orbit is

    seed:       (x,y)=(246,25),
    after Phi:  (x,y)=(88,97),
    after Phi^2:(x,y)=(105,119).

At the final point,

    F_+(105)=105^4+6*105^2+1 = 0 mod313.

Thus the earlier H17 one-Phi obstruction is genuinely broken by inserting the first nonlinear Phi. This is not merely a finite-field fantasy: it is the reduction of the exact rational `Phi^2` orbit.

### 3. Exact global squareclass certificate

Write `x2=A/B` in lowest terms and set

    G=A^4+6A^2B^2+B^4.

Then

    F_+(x2)=G/B^4,

so the squareclass of the final plus quartic is the squareclass of G. The exact 269-digit integer G is preserved in `results/exact-values.txt`, with

    SHA256(decimal G)
      = 5699f77e250e7f520056751fcaece47a8c83f8cf44cdf5da5f424910df3d73f8.

Exact division gives

    v_17(G)=2,
    v_137(G)=1,
    v_313(G)=1,
    v_1409(G)=1,
    v_2017(G)=1,

and

    v_2(G)=v_13(G)=v_257(G)=0.

Trial division through the square root independently certifies that 137,313,1409,2017 are prime. In particular **313 occurs to odd valuation, so it is genuinely in the output squareclass; but 137 also occurs to odd valuation, so the squareclass is not 313 alone.** This is the first exact two-Phi certificate in this route that introduces the previously obstructed target prime 313.

### 4. Rational rank information of the composite output

The final surface point gives two rational quartic witnesses for parameter G:

    F_+(x2)=G*u_+^2,       u_+=1/B^2,
    F_-(y2)=G*u_-^2,       u_-=W2*B^2/G.

For `e=+1,-1`, the inherited H8/H15 map

    X_e=G(t^2+e)^2/(4t^2),
    Y_e=G^2*u_e*(t^4-1)/(8t^3)

produces two exact rational points on

    E_G: Y^2=X^3-G^2X.

The Python checker and a separate Wolfram calculation verify both point equations exactly. The previously established Kummer-class calculation gives the two independent free classes `(G,1,G)` and `(1,G,G)` modulo the rational 2-torsion classes; removing the square factor of G gives the Q-isomorphic curve for its positive squarefree kernel n. Hence

    rank E_n(Q) >= 2.

This is a rank lower bound for a composite squareclass containing 137 and 313, not an exact rank, analytic-order certificate, or BSD result.

### 5. Verification and interpretation

`verify.py` uses only Python `Fraction` and exact integers. It reconstructs both Phi steps, the final surface identity, the complete mod313 reductions, the listed valuations, the explicit E_G points and their equations. Its accepted run passes.

A stateless exact Wolfram Language implementation independently reproduces the same fiber equality, residue orbit, digit counts, all eight listed valuations, the final surface residual and both E_G point equations, with no warnings.

H18 sharpens the prescribed-prime bottleneck: nonlinear transport can introduce 313 exactly, just as H15 introduced 257 as a factor, but the remaining problem is **cofactor removal**, not local target-prime creation. The next useful step is to modify the two-Phi orbit between its nonlinear stages and seek an exact output with odd 313-adic valuation but even 137-adic valuation, while separately tracking any new odd squareclass factors; no finite search alone can prove universal coverage.
