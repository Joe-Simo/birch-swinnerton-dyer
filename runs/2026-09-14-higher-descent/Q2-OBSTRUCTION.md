# We have not solved the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer problem yet.

## Exact Q_2 obstruction for d=2 and d=226

Use Fisher's authenticated first covering for E_113, cleared without changing its rational points:

    d r^2 = d^2 s^4 - 12769 t^4.

A Q_2 point may be scaled to a primitive integral triple (r,s,t) in Z_2^3, i.e. not all divisible by 2, because the equation is homogeneous of degree four if r is assigned weight two (equivalently scale s,t by a common denominator and r by its square). The following arguments show that no primitive triple exists for d=2 or d=226.

### d=2

The equation is

    2 r^2 = 4 s^4 - 12769 t^4.

Modulo 2, t must be even. With t even, the right side modulo 4 is 0, so 2 r^2 is 0 mod4 and r is even. Primitivity then forces s odd. Reduce modulo 16. Since t is even, t^4=0 mod16; since s is odd, s^4=1 mod4, hence 4s^4=4 mod16. Therefore the right side is 4 mod16. But for even r, 2r^2 is either 0 or 8 mod16. Contradiction. Thus C_2(Q_2)=empty.

### d=226=2*113

The equation is

    226 r^2 = 226^2 s^4 - 12769 t^4.

Again modulo 2, t is even. If s is odd, the first term on the right has exact 2-adic valuation 2, while the second has valuation at least 4; hence the right side has exact valuation 2. The left side has valuation 1+2 v_2(r), which is odd, so equality is impossible.

If s is even as well, primitivity forces r odd. Then the left side has valuation exactly 1, whereas both terms on the right have valuation at least 4, again impossible. Thus C_226(Q_2)=empty.

Wolfram independently checked the decisive residues: for d=2 the possible left residues with r even are {0,8} mod16 while the right side with s odd,t even is always 4 mod16; v_2(226^2)=2 and v_2(12769*2^4)=4.

## Consequence for the first Fisher/isogeny stage

From the previous real-place argument, all negative supported squareclasses are excluded. The classes 1 and 113 have explicit rational points: (s,t,r)=(1,0,1) for d=1 and (1,1,0) for d=113. The two remaining positive classes 2 and 226 are now excluded at Q_2. Subject to the already-authenticated support reduction to squareclasses generated by -1,2,113 for this isogeny descent, the everywhere locally soluble first-stage subgroup is therefore exactly {1,113}, a group of order 2.
