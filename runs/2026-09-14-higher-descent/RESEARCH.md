# We have not solved the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer problem yet.

## Checkpoint 002: an explicit higher-descent obstruction at 113

**Result.** For the specific curve A: y²=x³−113²x, the calculation below gives an algebraic rank-zero certificate and proves Sha(A/Q)[2∞] ≅ (Z/4Z)². The argument does not use an L-value, a BSD-predicted Sha order, or an initial assumption that Sha is finite. The independently computed Tunnell coefficient a(113)=8 then supplies the analytic rank-zero side, as a separate input.

This is a known special case, not a new resolution of BSD or a claim of global novelty. The new work in this checkpoint is the explicit covering, norm-tangent function, all-place certificate and higher-pairing calculation. The original target remains equality of algebraic rank and complex analytic order for every elliptic curve over Q, at every rank. No complete candidate exists. The proof below has undergone self-review and exact checks in two symbolic engines, not formal-kernel or independent expert verification.

Previous evidence remains unchanged in research/checkpoint-001.md and its archive. Its E113 rank-zero conclusion used analytic nonvanishing; that conclusion is not a premise of the algebraic proof here. Its E41 certificate is reused only after the E113 calculation, for comparison. ## 1. Models, isogenies and prior finite-descent input

Put p=113. Let

    A: y²=x³−p²x,             B: v²=u³+4p²u,
    phi: A -> B,              hatphi: B -> A.

The origins are at infinity, the field is Q, and the kernels of these rational 2-isogenies are generated by (0,0) on the respective models. On their affine domains,

    phi(x,y) = (x−p²/x, y(1+p²/x²)),
    hatphi(u,v) = ((u+4p²/u)/4, v(1−4p²/u²)/8).

They extend to the smooth projective curves, and hatphi phi=[2]. The checker verifies the equation and both coordinates of the composition, with rational functions reduced using the equation of A. The discriminants are 64p⁶ and −4096p⁶, respectively. Neither model is singular; no unidentified minimal-model transformation is used.

Use Fisher's [HF, §2] notation with E=A and E'=B. Thus S1=Sel^phi(A), S1'=Sel^hatphi(B). The group S_(2n) is the image of Sel_(2^n)(B) in S1; S_(2n+1) is the image of Sel^(2^n phi)(A). The primed groups swap A and B. These are F2 vector spaces. Their rational-point subgroups lie in every level.

The following preliminary facts use only descent, not analytic rank:

* S1 is contained in the squareclass span of 2 and p. For B the x-coordinate squareclass has support dividing 4p² and is positive at the real place. S1' is exactly the span of −1 and p: its possible support divides −p², and A's rational torsion points realize all four classes. In particular dim S_m'=2 for every m. The support restriction follows directly from the equation: at a prime not dividing b in y²=x³+ax²+bx with integral a,b, a nonzero x-coordinate cannot have odd valuation, whether its valuation is positive or negative. Apply this to each local Kummer image.
* dim Sel2(A)=4. Besides the previous Monsky reference, this now has the direct prime-family source [ETT, Proposition 2.3, printed p.6], whose proof enumerates the local images. ETT orders the coordinates as (x+p,x,x−p); the previous checkpoint used (x−p,x+p,x). Only the dimension, which is invariant under this permutation, is used here. This is an established external theorem, not a new full implementation of general 2-descent.
* The first Cassels pairing on Sel2(A) is zero. On its pure basis the off-diagonal value is (−mu/p) with p=2mu²−tau² and mu≡p mod4, by the computation in [Z, proof of Theorem 3.2, printed p.7]. Here (mu,tau)=(9,7), so (−9/113)=+1. The two diagonal entries are zero in additive notation, and rational torsion classes are in the radical. The reduction counts in §5 independently exclude rational 4-torsion, so the Kummer image of A[2](Q) has dimension two. For this prime the Rédei matrices are the zero one-by-one matrix for p and the zero two-by-two matrix for −p, yielding the hypotheses h4(p)=0 and h4(−p)=1. The norm divisor is d=p; p=7²+8² verifies the needed norm condition, since (p/7)²−p(8/7)²=p. The symbol computation in the proof is used even when it is +1; the theorem's nondegeneracy equivalence is not incorrectly used to infer positive rank.

The first-pairing radical theorem [HF, §1; Y, §4 and Theorem 6.3] therefore makes Sel4(A)->Sel2(A) surjective. In the exact sequence in the proof of [HF, Lemma 2.2] for n=2,

    dim Im(Sel4(A)->Sel2(A)) = dim S3 + dim S4'.

Here A has full rational 2-torsion: the preceding torsion terms have dimensions 1,2,1 and cancel. The left side is four and dim S4'=2, so **dim S3=2**, and hence

    S3 = S1 = <2,p>.

This supplies membership of both pairing arguments in S3 without presupposing rank zero or finiteness of Sha.

## 2. Constructing a locally soluble lift of the class 2

Fisher's §5 uses the opposite naming of the two curves. In that section take its E to be our B, and its E' to be the model y²=x³−16p²x, identified with our A by (x,y)=(4X,8Y). Its displayed isogeny out of B is our hatphi. This translation is essential.

Start with xi1=2 and

    C1: r²=2s⁴+2p²t⁴.

A conic parametrization is

    f(l,m)=p(−l²+2lm+m²),
    g(l,m)=l²+2lm−m²,
    h(l,m)=2p(l²+m²),
    2f²+2p²g²=h².

The initial choice xi2=1 was rejected: exhaustive primitive residues modulo 16 give no solution of f=s², g=t². A Q2 point would admit primitive integral coordinates and hence such a residue solution. This was a failed twist, not a failure of descent or a counterexample to BSD. The finite checker reproduces the rejection; no empty unbounded point search is used.

The successful choice is xi2=2, xi3=1. Define

    p1(c,d)=15c²+52cd+30d²,
    p2(c,d)=11c²+8cd+22d²,
    p3(c,d)=113(c²−2d²),

    q1(theta,psi)=theta²+2psi²,
    q2(theta,psi)=theta²+4theta psi+2psi²,
    q3(theta,psi)=theta²−2psi².

The identities

    f(p1,p2)=2p3²,       g(q1,q2)=2q3²

hold as exact polynomials. The coefficient matrices of (f,g,h), (p1,p2,p3) and (q1,q2,q3) in the corresponding binary quadratic monomial bases have respective nonzero determinants −204304, 204304 and −16. Thus these are genuine conic parametrizations (invertible linear transforms of the Veronese map), not merely identities from a constant or multiple-cover map. Set C=C3 in projective coordinates (c:d:theta:psi) by

    Q1 = p1−q1 = 0,
    Q2 = p2−q2 = 0.

It maps to C2: f(l,m)=2s², g(l,m)=2t² by l=p1=q1, m=p2=q2, s=p3, t=q3. On C2 take r=h/2 to map to C1. Finally C1 maps to B by

    u=2(s/t)²,       v=2rs/t³.

As in [HF, §5, diagram (14)], these extend to the smooth projective models. Their composite C->B is a **2phi-covering of degree eight** with Jacobian A. It is not a full [8]-covering, which would have degree 64. This distinction also fixes the index of the pairing below.

Let M_i be the symmetric matrix of Q_i, namely half its Hessian. Exact computation gives

    det(aM1+bM2) = −4p(a⁴−6a²b²+b⁴),
    disc_a det(aM1+M2) = 2²⁶ p⁶.

The pencil has four distinct singular members over an algebraic closure, so C is a smooth geometrically integral genus-one complete intersection; see [HF, §7, immediately after (23)]. It has good reduction at every odd prime other than p. Section 4 proves local solubility at every place, not merely at a sample of primes. Thus C supplies a class in Sel^(2phi)(A) above 2 in S3.

## 3. The pushout function, including its normalization

Work over L=Q(rho), rho²=2, and set z=c−rho d. The binary forms satisfy

    p1−(1+rho)p2 = (4−11rho)z²,
    q1−(1+rho)q2 = −rho[theta²+(4+2rho)theta psi+2psi²].

Consequently C maps to the L-conic

    H: theta²+(4+2rho)theta psi+2psi²−(11−2rho)z²=0.

It has the exact point (theta,psi,z)=(-1+rho,rho,1). Its half-gradient tangent form is

    ell=(1+3rho)theta+3rho psi−(11−2rho)z
       =(theta−11c−4d)+rho(3theta+3psi+2c+11d).

This is a tangent construction, not an arbitrary rational function selected for its local signs. To check it explicitly, put

    B0=4+2rho, A0=11−2rho,
    alpha=1+3rho, beta=3rho,
    q=lambda²+B0 lambda mu+2mu²,
    L0=alpha lambda+beta mu,
    Theta=(-1+rho)q−2lambda L0,
    Psi=rho q−2mu L0, Z=q.

Then H(Theta,Psi,Z)=0 and ell(Theta,Psi,Z)=−2L0². Hence the normalized tangent −ell/2 pulls back to a square. Its norm differs from the raw norm by 1/4, itself a rational square. Applying the pushout formula at the end of [HF, §5, printed p.13] therefore gives

    f4 = F/psi²,
    F = (theta−11c−4d)²−2(3theta+3psi+2c+11d)².

The formula belongs to the next 2-isogeny step C4->C3. Equivalently it is the pushout function for the rational point in A[phi]. General rational rescaling would not change the global product, by Hilbert reciprocity; here even the normalization factor is explicitly a square.

Expanded,

    F=113c²−46c theta−24c psi−226d²
      −140d theta−132d psi−17theta²−36theta psi−18psi².

The local formula in [HF, §3, printed p.7] computes

    Theta3(2,p) = product_v (f4(P_v),p)_v,

in multiplicative sign notation, with P_v a local point avoiding the zeros and poles. It pairs our lift in Sel^(2phi)(A) against the class p in Sel^phi(A). The pairing theorem, not a similarity of formulas, is what relates this product to rational points and Sha.

## 4. All-place local certificate

### The place p=113

Use the residue point (c,d,theta,psi)=(0,1,14,16). Direct integer evaluation gives

    (Q1,Q2)=(-678,-1582)=(-6p,-14p),
    F=-20302 ≡38 mod p.

The determinant of the theta,psi Jacobian minor is −2528≡71 mod p. Multivariate Hensel lifting, with c=0,d=1 fixed, supplies an actual Qp point in this residue class. Both F and psi are units. Thus

    (f4(P_p),p)_p = (38/113) = −1.

One checker enumerates the nonzero squares modulo 113; the other independently uses JacobiSymbol. Neither computation infers an exact zero from a floating approximation.

### The place 2 and the real place

Set c=d=1 and psi=−14/theta. Both equations reduce, after multiplication by theta², to the negative of

    H2(T)=T⁴−97T²+392.

At T=5, H2(5)=−1408 has 2-adic valuation seven and H2'(5)=−470 has valuation one. The strict Hensel inequality 7>2 holds, so there is an actual 2-adic unit root (indeed congruent to 5 modulo 64). The negative sign in psi=−14/theta is essential and is a negative-test fixture.

The numerator of T²F(1,1,T,−14/T) is

    N(T)=−17T⁴−186T³+391T²+2184T−3528.

It is coprime to H2; their resultant is 28692262021513216, not zero. Thus F is nonzero at the chosen root, and psi is nonzero. Since 113≡1 mod8, 113 is a square in Q2, so the 2-adic Hilbert contribution is +1.

For a real point take theta²=(97+sqrt(7841))/2 and psi=−14/theta. The discriminant is positive and less than 97², so this gives a real point; the same coprimality excludes zeros of F. The second Hilbert entry 113 is positive, giving contribution +1.

They lift by Hensel. Both entries of (F/psi²,113)_q are units at these odd primes, so every contribution is +1. A nonsquare unit does not change this unit-unit Hilbert rule.

For every prime q>=23 other than 113, the discriminant computation proves smooth reduction. The Hasse–Weil bound for a smooth geometrically integral genus-one curve is

    #C(Fq) >= q+1−2sqrt(q) >12.

The strict inequality holds at 23, since (23−11)²>4*23, and the left expression increases thereafter. The zeros of a nonzero quadratic section F on the degree-four curve contain at most eight geometric points; the hyperplane psi=0 contains at most four. These are the degree bounds from Bézout, including intersection multiplicity.

Neither section vanishes identically: C is not contained in a hyperplane, and the mixed coefficients of F in c theta,c psi,d theta,d psi are −46,−24,−140,−132, with gcd 2. At every odd prime at least one survives; Q1 and Q2 have no mixed terms of these types. Since the degree-two part of the complete-intersection ideal is their span, F is not identically zero on the reduced curve. Thus there is an Fq point outside both divisors. Smoothness and Hensel give a local point with F and psi units, and its Hilbert contribution is +1.

This proves coverage of every remaining prime. The finite list was not promoted to an infinite assertion: the pencil, degree bounds and Hasse–Weil bound supply the bridge. The invoked Hensel, Bézout and Hasse–Weil theorems are standard established external inputs; their general proofs are not formalized in this project.

Putting all places together,

    Theta3(2,113) = −1.

## 5. Rank and the full 2-primary obstruction group

By [HF, Theorem 2.1], Theta3 is alternating on the two-dimensional space S3=<2,p>, with kernel S4. Its nonzero off-diagonal entry gives the matrix

    [0 1]
    [1 0]

over F2. It is nondegenerate, so S4=0. Since B(Q)/phi A(Q) lies in S4 and dim A(Q)/hatphi B(Q)=2, the isogeny rank formula in [HF, §2] yields

    rank A(Q) = 0+2−1−1 = 0.

This conclusion did not use analytic nonvanishing. Direct good-reduction point counts are #A(F3)=4 and #A(F5)=4; the discriminant is prime to both. Prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion injectivity, applied at both primes, bounds rational torsion by four. The four visible 2-torsion points realize that bound, so A(Q)=(Z/2Z)².

The Selmer exact sequence now gives dim Sha(A)[2]=2. Also S1 -> Sha(A)[phi] is an isomorphism because the rational-point quotient B(Q)/phi A(Q) is zero. Hence

    Sha(A)[phi] = Sha(A)[2].

Indeed the left side has dimension two and is contained in the right side, since hatphi phi=[2]. The earlier zero first pairing means multiplication by 2 maps Sha(A)[4] onto Sha(A)[2]. Its kernel is Sha(A)[2], so Sha(A)[4] has order sixteen and is (Z/4Z)².

It remains to exclude order eight and higher; merely knowing Sha[4] would not do that. Each S3 class lifts to an element x of Sha(A)[2phi], which is a subgroup of Sha(A)[4]. Suppose a∈Sha(A) had order eight. Then 4a would be a nonzero element of Sha(A)[2]=Sha(A)[phi], but for every such lift x,

    <x,4a> = <4x,a> = 0.

This contradicts the zero right radical of Theta3 on all of Sha(A)[phi]. Thus no element of order eight exists. Any element of higher 2-power order would have a multiple of order eight, so none exists either. In particular there is no nonzero divisible 2-primary subgroup. We obtain

    Sha(A/Q)[2∞] = Sha(A/Q)[4] ≅ (Z/4Z)².

No finiteness of the full Sha, nor of its odd-primary parts, has been established or assumed. The Cassels–Tate functoriality and divisibility statements used here are explicitly independent of Sha-finiteness in [HF, Theorem 3.1 and its proof].

### Executed follow-up: the finite stopping principle and Sel8 comparison

The preceding exclusion is a general elementary criterion. If a 2-primary torsion group G has a bilinear pairing to Q/Z and a subgroup H⊂G[2^m] whose pairing with G[2] has zero right kernel, then G=G[2^m]. Otherwise an element a of order 2^(m+1) would give the nonzero element 2^m a∈G[2], orthogonal to every h∈H because 2^m h=0. Higher-order elements reduce to this case. This is not a new theorem claim, and it does not assert that such an H can always be found for an elliptic curve.

The rational torsion and Sha structure proved above imply, for every m>=1,

    |Sel_(2^m)(E113)| = 4*2^(2 min(m,2)).

The previous, separately algebraic E41 certificate gives rank two, rational 2-primary torsion (Z/2Z)² and Sha[2∞]=0. Thus

    |Sel_(2^m)(E41)| = 4*2^(2m).

They agree at Sel2 and Sel4 (orders 16 and 64), but **Sel8 has order 64 for E113 and 256 for E41**. This is a proved distinction using the deeper obstruction, not a guess from a BSD-predicted order. No abstract-group decomposition of Sel8 beyond its order is asserted.

The separate analytic check remains a(113)=8 and L(E113,1)=16 beta/sqrt(113)>0 by [T]. Hence its analytic order is zero, agreeing with the now independently obtained algebraic rank. This validates this known example, not the universal conjecture. A polynomial coefficient equal to zero, such as at 41, still does not certify its exact analytic order.
